The risk level, also called alpha, is the chance that the test flags a difference due to random variation when there is no real difference. At 10% risk, that would happen in about 10 out of 100 comparable tests where no real difference exists.

Choose **10%** for a practical starting point when comparing shot-to-shot variation. Choose **5%** or **1%** when a false positive would be more costly. At the same sample size, lower risk requires a larger difference to cross the decision limits. Check the sample-size options after changing the risk.

**Example**

For a two-factor test with 32 two-shot groups, the displayed band is **±22%** at 10% risk and **±26.1%** at 5% risk.
